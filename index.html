Served 150 full meals (~450 plates) in a single day at a Yoga Day event in our area. Zero complaints. Total sell-out.

My wife and I started Mithahara on a roadside stall from absolute zero — no investors, no backing, just a recipe we believed in and a gap we saw in our area: nobody was serving healthy, hygienic and sustainable breakfast at an accessible price. Within six months we had four employees, seven dishes with dedicated fan bases for each, and over ₹1 lakh in monthly revenue operating only Monday to Friday.

We built something people genuinely loved. Customers called us, came to our home when we were on hiatus, and still ask when we’re coming back. We received investment interest from multiple individuals but turned them all down — we wanted partners with skin in the game through effort, not just capital. Every class of person ate with us. Simple things, done elegantly.

Our values weren’t just a tagline. We used eco-friendly, biodegradable packaging. We paid salaries our employees couldn’t match working nine-hour days elsewhere. We believed money should be the last reason a person shows up to work. The business is on hiatus for family reasons — we are planning our comeback and building toward making Mithahara a global brand, pioneering healthy and sustainable eating.

★ Best Moment

Our office placed a bulk order of ~120 cups and we sold out completely at a local football event. Proof the product worked when it found the right crowd.

After the breakfast stall went on hiatus, a close friend — a serious fitness enthusiast who had been deep in nutritional research — brought me an overlooked insight: the fitness world obsesses over protein while under-indexing on fiber, which is equally critical for digestion and making protein absorption actually work. His idea: sell fresh fruit cups packed with at least 35g of fiber per serving, making it easy for people to hit their daily fiber goals in one go.

We sourced fruits directly from the Mandi — guava, apple, green apple, dragon fruit, kiwi, and pomegranate — and assembled cups priced at ₹60–80. The product was genuinely good. The challenge: we were trying to educate a market that wasn’t yet asking the question. Conversion was hard. Many people hadn’t heard of a daily fiber goal, and the price felt steep without that context. We didn’t crack it this time.

I remain a strong believer in the idea. The world is moving fast toward fiber-aware, gut-health-conscious eating. We just needed to meet people where their awareness already was, rather than starting the conversation from scratch. With the right marketing approach — leading with benefits people already care about — I’d back this again.
